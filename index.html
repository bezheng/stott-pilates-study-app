Study Guide

Key concepts from your STOTT PILATES materials.

The 5 Basic Principles
Core of STOTT method
1. Breathing — Inhale through nose, exhale through mouth. 3D breath into posterior & lateral rib cage. Activates transversus abdominis.
2. Pelvic Placement — Neutral (natural lordosis) or Imprint (slight posterior tilt, obliques shortened, no glutes).
3. Rib Cage Placement — Breathe into posterior/lateral ribs. Abdominals prevent "popping."
4. Scapular Movement & Stabilization — Attaches only to clavicle. 6 movements. Stabilize before every exercise.
5. Head & Cervical Placement — Natural cervical curve. Cranio-vertebral flexion initiates spinal flexion.
Pelvic Positions
Critical distinction
Neutral
Natural lordotic lumbar curve. ASIS & pubic symphysis in horizontal plane. Closed kinetic chain.
Imprinted
Slight posterior pelvic tilt. Obliques shortened — NO glute activation. Open kinetic chain.
Breathing Key Points
Principle 1
Inhale: rib cage opens out and UP → spinal extension
Exhale: rib cage closes in and DOWN → spinal flexion
Deep exhale activates transversus abdominis
Pelvic floor contraction fires transversus abdominis
3D breath = anterior, lateral, AND posterior rib cage

Muscle Contraction Types
Essential for written exam
Concentric
Muscle shortens while producing force. Prime mover actively shortening against resistance.
Eccentric
Muscle lengthens while producing force. Controls deceleration. Common in lowering phases.
Isometric
No change in length. Stabilization holds during Pilates exercises.

Practical Exam Evaluation Criteria
10 scored areas
1. Postural Analysis — Full PA, all sections, palpation + visual
2. Stated Focus of Workout — Clear focus with muscular & movement requirements
3. Five Basic Principles — All points covered; what, why, how
4. Programming Skills — Based on stated focus; well-rounded workout
5. Ability to Cue — Imagery, verbal, kinesthetic; client-specific
6. Ability to Correct — Combination of cue types; dynamically stabilized movement
7. Ability to Modify — Appropriate modifications; props used well
8. Knowledge of Content — Exercises, breath patterns, minimum numbers per level
9. Teaching Manner & Energy — Rapport, attitude, motivation
10. Rhythm & Pace — Fluidity, transitions, appropriate timing
